Step one, once

Decide what counts before you watch anything.

Most rating apps hand you one box and a number out of five. Your group sets weights instead: how much Story is worth against Pacing, whether a soundtrack can rescue a film. Agree once, and every film after that gets measured the same way.

Comedy night moves the goalposts, on purpose.

Pick a comedy and Humor arrives weighted heavier than Story. Pick a horror film and Fear Factor does the same. The rubric bends to the genre without anybody renegotiating it mid-argument, and every night keeps a snapshot of the rubric it was scored under, so old verdicts never quietly change meaning.

Not in the mood to tune anything? A group can run on Normies instead: Enjoyment, Acting, Writing. Three sliders, no configuration.

Step two, every time

Nobody sees a single score until everybody has locked one in.

The loudest person in the room usually sets the number. Blind scoring takes that away: no anchoring, no drifting toward whoever spoke first. The database enforces it, so it is not the app politely hiding things from you.

The disagreement is the feature.

Letterboxd is a diary: one person, one opinion, a stream of them. Mash Potato only makes sense with other people in the room. It keeps track of what your group agrees on, so after a few nights it can tell you who you actually share taste with, and it never ranks anybody against anybody.

One number, not an average of strangers

Mashed is your group's weighted result. A film can be a 4.1 in one group and an 8.6 in another, and both are correct.
